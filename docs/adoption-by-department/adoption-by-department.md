# Adoption by department

Usage analytics shows how an organization uses Lightdash as a whole. Adoption by department shows which parts of the organization are using it, including the parts that have not started, so enablement can be aimed where it is needed. Terms are defined in [CONTEXT.md](./CONTEXT.md).

The feature lives under Settings, Adoption (`/generalSettings/adoption`, and `/generalSettings/adoption/:departmentUuid` for one department). The index opens on a Map view with a List alternative. The choice is kept in the `view` query parameter and, as a fallback, in local storage per user.

## Access

All of the following must hold, otherwise the API answers 404 (no flag, no licence) or 403 (no scope). A malformed `departmentUuid` in the path is rejected earlier, by the route layer, with 422, so it returns 422 even when the feature is off:

- The `organization-adoption` feature flag is on for the user and organization (`FeatureFlags.OrganizationAdoption`).
- The instance has the enterprise licence. Without it the service is not registered and the controller turns the missing provider into a 404.
- The user holds `view:OrganizationAdoption` to read, and `manage:OrganizationAdoption` to change anything. `manage` depends on `view`, and `view` depends on `view:OrganizationMemberProfile`, so the custom role builder cannot make a role that sees members here but not on the members page. Organization admins get both; other roles need a custom role.

`view:OrganizationAdoption` shows every member's email, role and recent activity, and the names of the dashboards, explores, AI agents and groups they use, whatever the holder's own access to that content. Its description in the role builder says so: "View adoption by department, including every member's email, role and recent activity, and the names of the dashboards, explores, agents and groups they use". `manage:OrganizationAdoption` reads "Create and edit departments, their headcounts, targets, owners, linked groups and assigned people".

The routes are hidden from the generated API docs while the feature is under development.

## Data model

Three migrations in `packages/backend/src/database/migrations/` make the tables: `20261008120000_create_organization_departments.ts` creates the first four, `20261008150000_create_department_primary_memberships.ts` adds `department_primary_memberships`, and `20261008160000_allow_group_links_to_several_departments.ts` widens the `department_links` primary key so a group can be linked to several departments:

| Table                            | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `organization_departments`       | One row per department. `parent_department_uuid` makes the tree. Holds `headcount`, `headcount_note`, `target_active_users`, `target_date`, and `updated_by_user_uuid`. `headcount` and `target_active_users` cannot be negative (check constraints). Name is unique per organization whatever its case, through a unique index on `(organization_uuid, lower(name))`                                                                |
| `department_links`               | Groups linked to a department (`link_type = 'group'`; `space` and `project` are allowed by the check constraint but reserved). The primary key is `(link_type, link_uuid, department_uuid)`, so a group can be linked to several departments; it leads with the group, so finding a group's departments still uses it. Rolling the key back keeps each group's link with the lowest department uuid and logs how many links it drops |
| `department_members`             | People assigned to a department by hand. The primary key is `(department_uuid, user_uuid)`, and a person can be assigned to several departments. `DepartmentModel.setMembers` replaces one department's list and leaves the person's assignments to other departments as they are                                                                                                                                                    |
| `department_owners`              | Owners of a department, each a user or a group, ordered by `position`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `department_primary_memberships` | The one department a person in several departments counts in, their primary, chosen by someone with `manage`. The primary key is `(organization_uuid, user_uuid)`, so a person has at most one per organization. Deleting the department, the user or the organization deletes the row, so the person counts in every department again                                                                                               |

Every foreign-key column is covered by an index. `organization_departments.organization_uuid`, `department_members.department_uuid`, `department_owners.department_uuid` and `department_primary_memberships.organization_uuid` are covered as the leading column of a unique index or primary key and have no index of their own; the other referencing columns each have one.

`link_uuid` and `principal_uuid` are polymorphic and have no foreign key. Reads inner-join the target table, so a deleted group or user drops out. Deleting an organization cascades to its departments.

Where people are and where they count is worked out on every read, from the assignments and the linked groups. The only thing stored about it is a person's primary.

## Rules

### Tree

Departments form a tree. A department with no parent is at the top. A department cannot be its own parent or sit under one of its descendants; `DepartmentModel.update` rejects both with a 400. Deleting a department moves its sub-departments up one level, to the deleted department's parent. The foreign key is `ON DELETE SET NULL` only as a fallback.

Every department write (create, edit, delete, setting linked groups, assigned people or owners, and setting a person's primary) runs in one transaction that first takes a per-organization advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtextextended('organization-departments:<organization uuid>', 0))`). The existence, parent and cycle checks then read the organization's tree inside that transaction, before the write. Writes in one organization therefore run one at a time, so two moves at the same moment cannot store a cycle. The lock is released on commit or rollback, and writes in different organizations do not wait for each other. Before asking for the lock, the transaction sets `SET LOCAL lock_timeout = '5s'`: a write that waits longer than that behind other writes to the same organization is cancelled (Postgres code 55P03) and answers 409 with `ConflictError` ("Another change to departments is being saved. Try again in a moment"), so a burst of writes cannot hold the whole connection pool. The setting is `SET LOCAL`, like the activity reads' statement timeout, so it ends with its transaction and never stays on a pooled connection.

The tree helpers (ancestors, descendants, cycle check, depth, effective headcount, roll-up) are pure functions in `packages/common/src/departments/departmentTree.ts`. They walk the tree with explicit stacks and queues, never recursion, so a deep tree cannot overflow the call stack.

### Limits

`DEPARTMENT_TREE_LIMITS` in `DepartmentService.ts` and the owner check there keep every organization's tree small enough for the walks, the responses and the map to stay fast:

- At most 1,000 departments per organization.
- At most 10 levels deep. A top-level department is at level 1. Moving a department moves its whole branch, so after the move every department in the branch must still be within 10 levels.
- At most 20 owners per department, each counted once.

Creating or moving a department past the first two limits, or setting more owners, answers 400 with a plain message. The department count and depth are checked inside the per-organization lock described above, so departments created at the same moment cannot together pass the limit. The drawer's owner picker stops at 20.

A department's overlaps are worked out in memory from the cached snapshot, so they stay within the same bounds. Their `with` and `without` lists each name at most two departments (`MAX_OVERLAP_DEPARTMENTS` in common's `departmentInput.ts`), as the diagram draws at most three sets.

### Membership: placements and where people count

`resolveDepartmentMembership` in `packages/common/src/departments/resolveDepartmentMembership.ts` works out which departments each person is in and which they count in:

1. A person's placements are every department they are assigned to by hand (source `explicit`) and every department one of their groups is linked to (source `group`, naming the first such group by name). An assignment adds a placement beside the groups rather than overriding them, and a person can be assigned to several departments.
2. Where one placement is above another in the tree, the one above is dropped: a sub-department beats its parent, for assignments and groups alike. The departments of a stored cycle, which the writes refuse but older data could hold, are all kept.
3. What is left gives the person's `kind`: no placement is `unassigned`, one is `assigned`, two or more is `shared`. The API sorts `placements` by department uuid; the pages sort them by name.
4. A shared person counts in every one of their placements (`countedDepartmentUuids`), unless they have a primary that is one of them: then they count there alone, and their other placements stay visible as "also in". A primary that is not one of the person's placements is ignored.

Linking a group to a department leaves its links to other departments, so everyone in a group linked to two departments is placed in both. Setting a department's assigned people leaves their assignments to other departments.

Internal users (`users.is_internal`) are left out before placements are worked out, and cannot be members or owners.

A person counts as on Lightdash only when their user is active (`users.is_active`) and they have completed sign-up. There is no column for a pending invite, so sign-up is derived the way the organization members list derives it (`UserModel.findIfUsersHaveAuthentication`): the person has a password, a single sign-on identity, or a verified primary email. People provisioned through SCIM count from the moment they are provisioned, because SCIM verifies their email on create; this is intended, and lets an organization that provisions everyone through SCIM read coverage without maintaining headcounts by hand. Deactivated users and people invited but not yet joined do not count. `DepartmentModel.getResolvedMemberRows` leaves everyone else out, so they are not members, are neither unassigned nor shared, and fall into "no account" through the headcount.

Only people on Lightdash can be newly assigned to a department or made an owner. Assigning, or making an owner of, someone who is deactivated or has not completed sign-up answers 400 saying the person must be an active member; `DepartmentModel` checks it with the same definition as above, inside the write's transaction. People already assigned, or already owners, stay when they are later deactivated, so a department's list can still be saved with them in it; they stop counting until they are active again. The drawer's people pickers offer only people on Lightdash, plus anyone already chosen.

A department's own people are those who count in it. Its members are its own people plus those of all its descendants, each person once: the roll-up is a union (`rollUpByDepartment` in `departmentTree.ts`), so a person in two of its sub-departments is one member of it. `metrics` on a department is the rolled-up figure; `directMetrics` counts only the people who count in that department itself.

### Primary department

A person in several departments can be given a primary, the one department they count in: `PUT /people/{userUuid}/primary` with `{ "departmentUuid": "<uuid>" }`, or `null` to clear it so they count in each department again. The department must be one of the person's placements, read fresh from the database rather than from the cached snapshot, or the request answers 400; a person who is not on Lightdash in the organization answers 404. The write runs under the per-organization lock and drops the organization's cached snapshot. Deleting the department deletes the primary, so the person counts in every department again. A primary is cleared by the write that stops the person counting there, inside the same lock: removing them from the department, unlinking the group that placed them there, or moving or deleting a department so that it is no longer one of their placements (`clearStalePrimaries` in `DepartmentModel`). Placed there again later, they count in each of their departments until someone chooses again, so a primary never comes back on its own.

### Headcount

A headcount only ever adds people without an account: it never counts fewer than the people on Lightdash. It counts heads, not placements, so a person in several sub-departments counts once in their parent. The children's total of a department is its children's people on Lightdash, each once, plus each child's people without an account (the child's effective headcount less its members). The first part is the department's rolled-up members less the people directly in it, as nobody counted directly in a department is also counted below it (`getChildrenHeadcount` in `departmentTree.ts`). Effective headcount is the larger of the department's own headcount when set and its children's total plus the people on Lightdash directly in it (for a department without children, its members). When nobody is shared, the children's total is the sum of the children's effective headcounts. A department with no headcount on it or below it therefore counts its members, including when one person is in two of its sub-departments; `hasHeadcount` is false for it, and its coverage, which would only read 100 %, is not shown: the pages ask for a headcount instead. `computeEffectiveHeadcounts` takes the rolled-up member counts, each person once, and applies the floor as the members plus the children's people without an account, which is the same total. When the own value of a department with children is below that total, `headcountBelowChildren` is true and the total counts instead; the list and the department page say "The headcount entered is below its sub-departments and its own people, so that total counts instead". A parent whose own headcount equals its children's total but has people directly in it is flagged too. A parent headcount of 5 for 5 people is not flagged when its two sub-departments hold 3 each with one person in both, although their effective headcounts add up to 6.

A department with children keeps a residual headcount for the people directly in it: its effective headcount less its children's total, and never fewer than those people (`getResidualHeadcount` in `departmentTree.ts`). The floor above makes the residual exactly what the effective headcount leaves over the children's total, so the children's total and the residual always add up to the effective headcount. Without children, the residual is the effective headcount. The API accepts any headcount from 0. The drawer's headcount field gives the least it can count: "At least N: its sub-departments and the people already on Lightdash" for a department with sub-departments, N being their total plus its own people on Lightdash (`getHeadcountFloor` in `utils/headcount.ts`, which works the total out from the department's figures and its sub-departments' as the server does), and "At least N, the people already on Lightdash" for one without. Each department also carries a free-text `headcountNote` (500 characters at most) for where the number came from.

### Owners

A department has an ordered list of owners, each a user or a group. The first is the display owner. Setting owners replaces the whole list and keeps the order sent.

## Metrics

Computed in `DepartmentService` and `departmentMetrics.ts` from existing tables. There are no events and no scheduled jobs.

The organization snapshot behind the summary and the department page (departments, everyone's placements, activity counts and weekly buckets) is cached in memory for 60 seconds per organization, for at most 500 organizations per backend process, dropping the oldest first. Requests that arrive together share one load, and a failed load is not kept. The department page reuses the cached snapshot and reads its own people with the snapshot's time windows, so its count and its member list still agree. Any write through `DepartmentService` drops that organization's snapshot in the process that handled it. Other backend processes keep their copy until it expires, so behind a load balancer the figures can be up to a minute old after a change, and changes made elsewhere (someone joining a linked group, new activity) show within a minute.

| Metric         | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Members        | People on Lightdash who count in the department or a descendant, each once. A person in several departments with no primary counts in each of them. Internal users, users without a primary email, deactivated users and people who were invited but have not completed sign-up are excluded                                                                        |
| Coverage       | Members divided by effective headcount, as a rounded percentage. Never above 100, as effective headcount is never below the members                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Active         | Members who ran a query themselves or through the AI agent or MCP (`query_history`, counted contexts only) or viewed a chart or dashboard (`analytics_chart_views`, `analytics_dashboard_views`) in the last 30 days, divided by effective headcount, so never above 100                                                                                            |
| Activity split | Members by their latest activity from the same sources as Active: healthy with activity in the last 30 days (the Active count), at risk with activity in the last 90 days but not the last 30, lost with none in the last 90 days or none ever. Each bound includes its first instant. `HEALTHY_ACTIVITY_DAYS` and `AT_RISK_ACTIVITY_DAYS` in common set the bounds |
| Role split     | Members by organization role. Member and viewer count as viewers, developer counts as editor. A role this feature does not know counts as a viewer and is logged once as a warning, so a new role cannot fail the summary                                                                                                                                           |
| Weekly active  | Distinct members with a chart or dashboard view in each of the last 12 weeks, oldest first. Queries are left out so that every week is counted the same way                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Shared         | `sharedCount`: of the members, those who also count in another department of the tree, so never someone with a primary. On a parent's `metrics` it includes a person in two of its own sub-departments; on `directMetrics` it is the department's own people who also count elsewhere                                                                               |
| Direct         | `directMetrics`: the same measures for the people who count in the department itself, with coverage and active as percentages of its residual headcount rather than its effective headcount                                                                                                                                                                         |

The summary's `organization` row counts everyone on Lightdash, in a department or not. `placed` counts the people who count in at least one department of the tree, each once, with their role and activity splits; the organization panel and the legend under the map read it. `attention` gives `unassignedCount`, the people on Lightdash in no department, and `sharedCount`, the people in more than one department with no primary. A shared person is information, not an error: they count in each of their departments until someone picks a primary.

A query counts only when a person asked for it. `DepartmentAnalyticsModel` keeps the `query_history` rows whose `context` the backend's `queryWorkloadOrigin` (`packages/backend/src/services/AsyncQueryService/queryUsage.ts`) classifies as interactive, plus the AI agent and every MCP context, because a person asking the agent or using MCP is adoption. Counted: dashboards, explores, saved charts and their history, SQL charts, the SQL runner, viewing underlying data, the metrics explorer, the AI agent and MCP. Not counted: API and CLI runs, scheduled deliveries and alerts as queries, Google Sheets syncs, auto-refreshed dashboards, embeds, and internal contexts such as filter autocomplete, downloads, totals and pre-aggregation. A unit test lists every query context as counted or not counted, so a new one has to be decided. The same filter applies to the per-person query count and to top explores.

Active has one definition. The summary's weekly buckets and each person's latest activity come from a single query (`DepartmentAnalyticsModel.getActivity`, which scans queries and views back 90 days and returns one row per person: their latest activity and the weeks they viewed a chart or dashboard in, of which the trend keeps its own). `getActivityBucket` in common (`departments/activityBuckets.ts`) puts each person in healthy, at risk or lost against the two instants of `ActivityWindows`, measured once per request by `getActivityWindows`, and the 30-day count is the healthy count, so the two never differ. The member list's `isActive30d` flag (`getMemberActivity`) uses the same sources and bounds and reads every source back 90 days only, and each member carries `activity`, the bucket the counts put them in. The browser reads the server's flag and bucket for the member filters, the map's dot colours and the legend; it never compares `lastActiveAt` to its own clock.

Every activity read is limited to the organization. `query_history` has an organization column and is filtered on it. `analytics_chart_views` and `analytics_dashboard_views` do not, so they are read by the organization's member set (the user uuids `DepartmentModel.getResolvedMemberRows` returns from `organization_memberships`) on their `(user_uuid, timestamp)` index. The member set is the tenancy boundary: a person belongs to one organization in the product, their activity is theirs whatever content it was on, and nobody outside the organization is ever in the set. One organization per person is enforced in application code, not by the schema: `UserModel.joinOrg` refuses a user who already has a membership, but `organization_memberships` has the primary key `(organization_id, user_id)` and no unique constraint on `user_id`, and `joinOrg` checks and inserts without a lock. A person who was in two organizations anyway would have their chart and dashboard views counted in both (last activity, 30-day dashboard views, the active flag and the weekly buckets), with no content names crossing, because queries and top content stay limited to the organization. The reads used to join each view to the organization through the content (chart to project, dashboard to space to project); on a real organization with 2.5 million chart views in 12 weeks that made Postgres scan once per chart and sort every view to disk, 5 s against 0.8 s for the member-set read. Top content still joins through the content, because it lists the organization's own dashboards, explores and agents by name.

Query history is not kept for long. The instance deletes `query_history` rows older than `QUERY_HISTORY_RETENTION_DAYS` (32 days by default, on unless `QUERY_HISTORY_CLEANUP_ENABLED` is `false`), while the two view tables are kept. Three things follow:

- The weekly trend counts chart and dashboard views only. A week 10 weeks back has no queries left to count, so counting queries in recent weeks would make them look busier than old ones. A person who only runs explores or SQL does not appear in the trend.
- The 30-day active count uses views and the queries a person ran. If the instance keeps queries for fewer than 30 days, the count misses the queries already deleted and reads low.
- `lastActiveAt` is the latest of a person's views and retained queries in the last 90 days. Null means nothing is recorded in those 90 days, not that the person never used the product, so the page says "No activity in 90 days".

Percentages are of effective headcount, so they never exceed 100, and they are null when it is 0. `directMetrics` percentages are of the residual headcount, so they never exceed 100 either, and they are null when the residual is 0.

Time handling is UTC, but not everything is calendar-aligned:

- Week buckets start on UTC Mondays, on both the SQL side (`date_trunc('week', ...)`) and the TypeScript side (`lastNWeekStarts`). The newest bucket is the current, partial week.
- The 30-day active window and the 90-day last-activity read are rolling windows measured from one instant (`getActivityWindows()` in common's `departments/activityBuckets.ts`: now minus 30 days and now minus 90 days), not from a UTC midnight. Every read in a request uses the same two bounds, and the 12-week trend takes its weeks from the 90-day read.
- `weeksLeft` and the frontend's "N days ago" label count UTC calendar days. The label is for display only and never decides who is active.

The timestamp columns read here are `timestamp without time zone`. They are read as UTC only if the server process and the database session run in UTC; the code does not enforce it.

The department page (`getDetail`) adds:

- Target progress: `targetActiveUsers` against current active members. `remaining` is never negative. `weeksLeft` is null with no target date, 0 on the target day, positive before it (rounded up, so a partial week counts) and negative once overdue. Targets stay in the API and the data model, but no page shows or edits them: the department page has no target tile, the list no target column, and the drawer no target fields.
- The weekly trend. The API still sends `orgAverage` (the mean across departments at the same depth), but the page no longer draws it: the dashed comparison line is "At the organization's rate", computed in the browser from the summary it already has as the organization's weekly active people divided by its people on Lightdash, times this department's people on Lightdash. A department with nobody on Lightdash has nothing to compare, so the line is left out and the page says "No comparison: nobody on Lightdash yet". The last point of every weekly series is the current, partial week, drawn hollow with a dashed segment and labelled "this week so far".
- Key content (`topContent` in the API) for the last 30 days, up to five each of dashboards, explores and AI agents, with use counts, distinct people and the project each one is in. The page shows it under "Key content" and links every name to its content.
- A member list sorted with no activity in 90 days first, then longest inactive. Each row has the person's last activity in the last 90 days, whether they are active in 30 days (`isActive30d`), queries and dashboard views in 30 days, and where they count under this department (`departmentUuid`: the department itself if they count there, else the first sub-department they count in). `isDirect` says whether that is this department or a descendant. `sharedWith` names their other placements in the tree, and `primaryDepartmentUuid` is their primary, null without one.

The AI agent read is guarded by a table check (`hasAiTables`) because `ai_prompt`, `ai_thread` and `ai_agent` come from enterprise migrations and do not exist on every instance. Without them the list is empty.

Every activity read (the summary's activity, the member activity and each top-content list) runs in its own transaction that first sets `SET LOCAL statement_timeout = 15000`, so a runaway read is cancelled after 15 seconds instead of holding a pooled connection. A cancelled read answers with `TimeoutError` ("Adoption figures took too long to load. Try again in a minute"), the closest existing error class: there is no 503 class in `@lightdash/common`, and `TimeoutError` answers 400.

## API

All routes are under `/api/v1/org/departments` in `packages/backend/src/ee/controllers/OrgDepartmentsController.ts`. The organization always comes from the session; no route accepts an organization identifier. A department that belongs to another organization answers 404, the same as one that does not exist.

| Route                            | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Scope  |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| `GET /`                          | Every department with rolled-up metrics, the organization's totals (`organization`, everyone on Lightdash, and `placed`, the people in a department), and the `attention` counts (`unassignedCount`, `sharedCount`) | view   |
| `GET /membership`                | Each person's placements, `kind`, primary and the departments they count in                                                                                                                                         | view   |
| `GET /{departmentUuid}`          | Department page data                                                                                                                                                                                                | view   |
| `GET /{departmentUuid}/overlaps` | The departments that share people with this one, the diagram of the largest two, and with `with` or `without`, the people in one region                                                                             | view   |
| `POST /`                         | Create                                                                                                                                                                                                              | manage |
| `PATCH /{departmentUuid}`        | Edit. An omitted field is unchanged, null clears it                                                                                                                                                                 | manage |
| `DELETE /{departmentUuid}`       | Delete                                                                                                                                                                                                              | manage |
| `PUT /{departmentUuid}/groups`   | Replace linked groups. A group keeps its links to other departments                                                                                                                                                 | manage |
| `PUT /{departmentUuid}/members`  | Replace the people assigned to this department by hand. Their assignments to other departments stay                                                                                                                 | manage |
| `PUT /{departmentUuid}/owners`   | Replace owners                                                                                                                                                                                                      | manage |
| `PUT /people/{userUuid}/primary` | Set the department a person counts in, or clear it with `null`                                                                                                                                                      | manage |

Each item of the department page's `topContent` (`DepartmentTopContentItem`) is `{ id, name, projectUuid, count, distinctPeople }`. `projectUuid` is the project the content is in: a dashboard's comes from its space, an explore's from the query, and an AI agent's from the agent. An explore's `id` stays `projectUuid:exploreName`, and queries whose project has been deleted are left out, as they have nothing to link to. The page builds each link only from these ids and the explore's name, URL-encoded: `/projects/{projectUuid}/dashboards/{id}/view`, `/projects/{projectUuid}/tables/{name}` and `/projects/{projectUuid}/ai-agents/{id}`, so a name can never become a URL of its own.

`GET /{departmentUuid}/overlaps` (`computeDepartmentOverlaps` in `departmentOverlaps.ts`) lists the departments that share people with a department D: every department neither above nor below D that holds people counted in D, rolled up, as `{ departmentUuid, name, people, active30d }`, most people first, then by name. Departments above and below D always hold its people, so they are never overlaps. `venn` draws D with its largest overlap and the largest overlap neither above nor below that one, so no set holds another, or with the largest alone when every other overlap is related to it; it is null when D has no overlap. Its `regions` are every combination of the sets, single sets first, each with `people` and `active30d`. Each person is in exactly one region, the combination of sets that holds them, so the regions add up to the union of the sets. `with` and `without`, comma-separated uuids, ask for D's people who are also in every `with` department and in no `without` one. They come back in `members`, in the department page's member shape, which is null when neither list is given; an empty list is the same as leaving it out. A list naming D or more than two departments, a department in both lists, or a department outside the organization answers 400, and a D that does not exist answers 404.

Validation in `DepartmentService`:

- Names are required and at most 255 characters. Before a name is checked or stored it is normalised: every code point Unicode marks Default_Ignorable is removed (the zero-width and bidirectional controls, the soft hyphen, the combining grapheme joiner, the variation selectors, the tag characters, the Hangul and Khmer fillers and the other format controls that render as nothing), then NFKC is applied, the name is trimmed, and every run of whitespace inside it is made one space. They are removed before NFKC so that an accent one of them kept apart from its letter still composes. A name of more than 1,020 characters as sent (four times the limit) is refused before any of this, because NFKC can make a name many times longer; the 255 limit applies to the normalised name. Names that only look alike therefore cannot sit side by side, and a name made only of invisible characters is refused as empty. Names are unique per organization regardless of case (409, so "finance" is refused when "Finance" exists).
- A name or headcount note containing a control character (U+0000 to U+001F, or U+007F to U+009F), such as a tab or a line break, answers 400. The drawer turns line breaks typed into the note into spaces before it sends the note, because the note always shows on one line.
- Headcount and target are whole numbers from 0. The target date is a real `YYYY-MM-DD` date in a year from 1900 to 2200.
- The path `departmentUuid` and `userUuid` must be UUIDs (422 from the route layer, before the flag and scope checks). Ids in request bodies (`groupUuids`, `userUuids`, owner `uuid`, `parentDepartmentUuid`, a primary's `departmentUuid`) and in the overlaps' `with` and `without` lists are checked with the helpers in common's `departmentInput.ts` and answer 400, as do unknown groups, users or parents. The primary and overlaps routes check theirs in the controller as well as in the service. Every uuid from a path or a body is lower-cased before it is used, so an upper-case uuid behaves exactly like the lower-case one.
- Input repeated back in an error message is cut to 80 characters and an ellipsis.

Response types are in `packages/common/src/types/departments.ts`.

## Frontend

Code is under `packages/frontend/src/ee/`:

- `pages/Adoption.tsx` is the index: the Map, List and Waffle views, the attention strip, and, for people with `manage`, the department drawer, the placement modal and the Place people button in the header that opens it.
- `pages/AdoptionDepartment.tsx` is one department's page. It checks the route's `departmentUuid` is a UUID before anything is fetched; any other value shows "Department not found" and requests nothing.
- `hooks/useOrgDepartments.ts` holds the query and mutation hooks. Mutations invalidate the `org-adoption` query key. Every department path segment is URL-encoded, and the detail query only runs for a UUID, so a crafted value cannot reach another API path.
- `features/adoption/map/` draws the map: departments as nested circles, people as dots coloured by activity or role. Layout and geometry are pure modules with tests.
- `features/adoption/waffle/` draws the Waffle view: departments as blocks, people as squares. Its layout, grouping and blocks are pure modules with tests.
- `features/adoption/components/` and `utils/` hold the list, drawer, tiles, charts and helpers.

The map sizes circles by effective headcount at every level: areas are proportional, the pack is computed from those radii, and siblings are packed largest first so a rename never changes a size. Sizes are exact within a department and only approximate across departments, because each department's children are scaled to fill it; the legend says so. A circle that would be too small to select is enlarged after packing, a department with sub-departments included (they scale with it), but never past a sibling with more people, so no circle is drawn smaller than one beside it with fewer people; it is flagged "not to scale" when the enlargement exceeds half a pixel. Each circle of the level in view (the top-level departments, or an open department's sub-departments and its "Directly in" circle) is named at rest: the name in bold over its shortest line of numbers, centred under the circle, over it when under it would leave the map, and right of the zoom buttons when it would sit on them (`getRestLabels` in `mapLayout.ts`). The names move and zoom with their circles at a constant size on screen, and a circle under 24 px across on screen is named on hover only. Nothing is moved or resized to make room for them: where two names would overlap, the larger circle keeps its name and the smaller one is named on hover only. They never take the pointer, and a halo keeps them readable where they cross another circle. Any circle's label also shows while the circle is hovered or its control has keyboard focus, and that is the only time a circle inside those of the level in view is named (a sub-department's name carries its count on one line). Hovering a circle of the level in view gives the fuller line, "187 of 420 on Lightdash · 115 active · 6 sub-departments", each part only where it applies, so it adds to the name at rest. A hover label takes the place of its circle's name at rest, and of any other name it would sit on, so labels never stack. Hovering the people directly in a department shows that department's name, and the panel beside the map lists the departments in view. Circles are packed as large as the area allows, less a band at the bottom that keeps the lowest circle's label inside the map (`HOVER_LABEL_BAND_PX` in `mapLayout.ts`: the gap, the label's two lines and the inset from the edge). A department opened on its own with no sub-departments is one circle of all its people, quoting its effective headcount. Beside sub-departments, the people directly in a department have a circle of their own, sized by the department's residual headcount and drawing a grey ring for each of it without an account: its hover label reads "Directly in Supply chain" over "50 of 80 on Lightdash · 12 active", and the panel lists them last under the sub-departments as "Directly in Supply chain · 50", with a bar over the residual and their coverage of it. The circle and the row read the residual from one helper, `getDirectHeadcount` in `utils/headcount.ts`. In a department with no headcount anywhere the residual is just those people: the label leaves out "of N" and the row says "Add headcount" or "No headcount", as other rows without a headcount do. A department with sub-departments that keeps no residual, with nobody directly in it, has no such circle or row. Drawn at the top of the view with nobody in it, the circle is dashed like an empty department and the legend keys it as "Nobody on Lightdash yet"; inside another circle it is a plain ring. Every department's circles therefore hold exactly its effective headcount, so the dots drawn of each kind, the legend, the panel and the map's screen-reader description count the same people, with one exception: a person who counts in several of the departments drawn has a dot in each, so there the dots outnumber the people on Lightdash, while the grey rings still add up to the people without an account. The legend, the panel and the description count that person once.

Color by offers two colourings, Activity (the default) and Role. Activity colours each person on Lightdash Healthy (activity in the last 30 days), At risk (activity in the last 90 days but not the last 30) or Lost (no activity in the last 90 days, or none ever), all as filled dots, in green, amber and red (red 7 in both schemes); people without an account stay the grey ring of No account. In the light scheme the green and amber dots and bar parts carry a darker edge of their own hue, which puts them 4.9:1 and 3.6:1 against the circle fill. Role keeps its violets, from the strongest for admins through editors and a lighter violet for interactive viewers to viewers, who are drawn as rings, in the bars as well as on the map. Every colour is a custom property defined once on the map's root in `AdoptionMap.module.css` (`--adoption-healthy`, `--adoption-at-risk`, `--adoption-lost` and their edges, `--adoption-admin`, `--adoption-editor`, `--adoption-interactive-viewer`, `--adoption-viewer` and `--adoption-no-account`, the grey of the No account ring and of the bars' track), which the dots, both legends and the panel's bars read. In both schemes the amber is lighter than the green and the red, and the green lighter than the red, so neighbouring buckets differ in lightness as well as hue. The legend under the map and the panel follow the colouring chosen, a person's dot is titled with their name and their part of the colouring ("Ada Lovelace · Healthy"), and the map's screen-reader description names the parts with their counts ("coloured by activity: 6 healthy, 0 at risk, 6 lost, 26 with no account"), or says "no headcount set" in place of the last where no headcount is entered in view, as the panel asks for headcounts there.

A person who counts in several departments has a dot in each of them, ringed. With people loaded (150 or fewer in view), a person is drawn in every department they count in, in their own department alone when they have a primary, and never twice in one (`groupMembersByDepartment` in `mapView.ts`). Drawn from counts, each circle rings as many of its dots as its `sharedCount` says (the people directly in a parent read its `directMetrics`), spread evenly through its people on Lightdash (`spreadEvenly` in `geometry.ts`), never a no-account dot, and the same dots in either colouring; which people they stand for is not known. The ring is 1 px wide at the edge of the room the dot has, with the dot drawn smaller inside it and a gap of up to 1 px, so it never reaches a neighbour (`getSharedDotShape` in `geometry.ts`); one helper in `DepartmentMap.tsx` sizes a dot and its ring. Its grey, `--adoption-shared-ring`, is defined with the other colours on the map's root. The rings are a layer of their own under the dots, outside the dots layer that the colour sweep reads as one circle per dot, and a loaded person's ring takes clicks and hovers as their dot does, so the whole room selects them. The legend keys the ring as "Counts in several departments" while one is drawn. Hover labels, circle controls and circle titles add "3 count in another department too": in the fuller line of a circle of the level in view, or on a second line under a sub-department's name, and it is the first thing a hover label leaves out for room; names at rest keep their shortest line. The map's description counts each person once, from the panel's numbers, adds how many also count in another department, and says "A person who counts in several departments has a ringed dot in each" while dots are drawn. The person selected reads "In 3 departments: Depots, Finance, Stores" (names sorted) and, with a primary, "Counts in: Stores"; a person in one department reads "Viewer · Stores". The hidden list of people on the map names each person once.

Changing the colouring sweeps the new colours across the map from its top-left corner: each dot fades to its new colour over 380 ms once the sweep's diagonal front reaches it, and one soft white band travels with the front. Only people's dots fade, and only when the colouring changes. New data, a zoom or a selection during a sweep shows at once and takes the band away, while the other dots still change on the sweep's timing; hovering never animates. For people who prefer reduced motion the colours change at once. `features/adoption/map/colourTransition.ts` holds the transition for the map and the Waffle view. Its other option, `reflow`, glides people to their new places in the Waffle view; on the map, whose dots never move, it is the sweep without the band.

The panel beside the map has a title, one bar and a list. With nothing open the title is "Organization" with "All departments" beside it; with a department open it is the department's name beside its parent's, or "Department" for a top-level one. The bar splits the people on Lightdash into the parts the map colours them by, healthy, at risk and lost with Activity, or admins, editors, interactive viewers and viewers with Role, over a track for the people without an account (effective headcount less the people on Lightdash, never below 0), with each part's count under it keyed with the dot the map draws for those people. The rows' bars follow the colouring too. Across the organization it counts the people placed in a department, not everyone on Lightdash: each person once from the summary's `placed`, which carries their activity and role splits so that either colouring counts a person in several departments once, plus each top-level department's people without an account (`getChildrenHeadcount` with the top-level departments as the children). When no headcount is entered in what the panel covers (in no department across the organization, or nowhere on the open department or below it), "Add headcounts to see coverage" takes the place of the no-account count. `getOrganizationBreakdown` and `getDepartmentBreakdown` in `utils/peopleBreakdown.ts` give the panel's numbers and the legend's under the map, one helper for both, and with the residual circles the dots drawn match them kind by kind wherever nobody is shared. The list, "Departments" or "Sub-departments", has a row per department in view, lowest coverage first (nobody counted reads 0, and the largest department leads among equals), each with a small bar over its effective headcount and, in a 96 px last column so the bars never change width, its coverage. A department with no headcount entered on it or below it, sub-departments or not, shows "Add headcount" there to people with `manage` and "No headcount" to everyone else, and sorts as 0; one with an effective headcount of 0 shows "Nobody yet" over an empty track. Selecting a row opens the department on the map, as selecting its circle does. An open department adds Open and, for people with `manage`, Edit department, and the panel shows the person selected on the map. On the organization map the legend says it counts people placed in a department, and the map's screen-reader description says its people on Lightdash are those placed in a department. There are no cards under the map; the attention strip above it carries the count of people waiting to be placed.

Coverage and Active in 30 days read as a share and a count, "56% (1,317)", in the department page's tiles and rows and the list, the map's panel gives coverage as a share alone, and none of them goes above 100 %. Where no headcount is entered on a department or below it, the department page's Coverage tile and sub-department rows and the map panel's rows show "Add headcount" to people with `manage` and "No headcount" to everyone else in place of the share, and the tile's caption gives the people on Lightdash. In the list, people with `manage` get "Add" in the Headcount column and an empty Coverage cell, and everyone else gets "–" and "No headcount". The map draws the department's circle dashed. The active caption gives the share of the headcount, and adds the share of the people with an account only when some of the headcount has none.

The role split ("131 viewers, 40 interactive viewers, 20 editors, 2 admins", leaving out roles nobody holds) shows in the list's Roles column, which is shown only when the table is at least 1,300 px wide; on the map, colouring by role gives the counts in view in the legend. The department page has a breadcrumb, its title and, for people with `manage`, Edit department, with no line of owners, headcount, linked groups or roles under the title: the coverage tile states the headcount, and owners, linked groups and the headcount note are in Edit department. Below the title the page shows two tiles, Coverage and Active in 30 days. The list gives the headcount note as a tooltip on the headcount that opens on hover, keyboard focus and touch.

The people table's chips are All, Active in 30 days, Not active in 30 days and No activity in 90 days. The list view never wraps a cell: names and owners truncate with the full text on hover, and numbers carry thousands separators. The attention strip says "12 people are in no department. They aren't counted in any department until you place them", with "place them" a link to the placement modal, and under it, muted, "3 people in more than one department count in each of them", a link to the modal's Shared tab. People without `manage` read the same sentences without links, the first ending "until an admin places them". Only people in no department make it a warning; people in several departments alone give a neutral note. The strip counts the people in more than one department with no primary, while the Shared tab lists everyone in more than one department. The placement modal, "Place people", has two tabs. Unassigned lists the people in no department, with a checkbox per row and "Place selected in", which sends one request per department carrying everyone selected; the number selected is announced to screen readers. Shared lists everyone in more than one department by name, with their departments as chips and a "Counts in" select, Everywhere or one of their departments, which sets or clears the person's primary. The drawer's assigned-people picker offers people already in other departments, and their chips say "also in Marketing".

The department page has an Overlaps section between Key content and Sub-departments, shown when a department outside this one shares people with it. It draws the department and its two largest overlaps as a diagram of up to three circles of one size, so the counts in its regions carry the sizes (from 10,000 in thousands, "12.3k"), beside a list of every overlapping department with its people and how many of them are active, most people first, which scrolls after eight rows. Selecting a row, or a region of the diagram that holds this department, lists those people in the people table below, with a chip that clears it: "Also in Marketing", "Also in Marketing, not in Sales" or "Not in Marketing or Sales". Regions without this department are counts only. A person's row says "Also in Marketing and Sales" under the department column; past three names it gives the first three and "and 2 more", with every name in its title.

The sidebar entry and the routes are added only when the instance has a valid enterprise licence (`health.license.valid`), the flag is on and the user has `view`. One helper, `canAccessOrganizationAdoption` in `packages/frontend/src/hooks/settings/organizationAdoptionAccess.ts`, decides both, so an unlicensed instance with the flag on shows no entry.

### Waffle view

Waffle is the third view in the switch, after Map and List, remembered and linked the same way (`view=waffle`). It draws the whole organization as one block per top-level department, largest first, sized by effective headcount, with one square per person. A block's header gives its name, cut short with an ellipsis when the block is narrow, and its counts line, "834 of 1,900 · 495 active": its people on Lightdash of its effective headcount, and how many were active in the last 30 days ("14 on Lightdash · 10 active" without a headcount). Its sub-departments are stacked inside it, each with everyone below it, and the people directly in the department come last as "Directly in Commercial", over the residual headcount it keeps for them, as on the map. A department without sub-departments is one area of all its people. A part taller than 30 px (label included) and wider than 100 px is labelled "Sales · 420 of 760". Each part holds one square per person of its headcount: the people on Lightdash in the colours of the current colouring, grouped in legend order so the part reads as a stacked bar, then a grey square for each person without an account. A block's parts hold exactly its effective headcount, and nobody is drawn twice.

Blocks share a row in proportion to their effective headcounts and are never narrower than 72 px. When the minimum widths no longer fit in one row, the blocks wrap to as few rows as hold them, the largest first, with rows that differ by at most one block, so no department sits alone on a row it could share. A part's squares are sized for the room the part would have if the waffle filled a 560 px drawing, the map's height, with rows and parts sharing it by headcount: clamp(3, floor(sqrt(area / people)), 10) px, 1 px apart under 5 px and 2 px apart from 5 px, made smaller until every row fits. When every part's people fit on one row of 16 px squares across their block, a small organization, the cap is 16 px instead of 10. The height then follows the content: each part is as tall as its rows of squares need and never shorter than 26 px, every block is as tall as the tallest one in its row, and the waffle is as tall as its rows, never under 160 px and with no maximum (the page scrolls). A company of 33 people in four departments, at 870 px, is one band of blocks 78 px tall with a row of 16 px squares in each, and the 6,000-headcount organization keeps the squares it had in the 560 px drawing in about 500 px. `waffle/layout.ts` works all of this out in pixels and every block, part and square is placed from it: squares are absolutely positioned and translated into place, one element each, not laid out by the page.

A part whose squares would be under 3 px is drawn as one horizontal stacked bar of its people in legend order, filling the part, which keeps the height its people's share of the 560 px drawing gives it. Above 20,000 people in view (the effective headcounts of the top-level departments added up) every part is a bar, and the legend says so.

Color by has the map's options (Active in 30 days, Role, Last active), colours and legend row; the breadcrumb and the Color by control are one component, `map/AdoptionViewHeader.tsx`, used by both views. The map's rings, for people not active and for viewers, are outlined squares, and people without an account are the grey of the panel's no-account swatch. The legend counts come from `getLegendCounts` over `getPeopleBreakdown`, the helpers behind the map's legend and its panel, so they equal the squares drawn of each kind. The map's panel sits beside the waffle. Selecting a block, or a sub-department's part, selects that department as selecting its circle on the map does: the panel shows it and the breadcrumb above the waffle names it and leads back up. Blocks and sub-department parts are buttons, so Enter selects them, and a click on the people directly in a department, or on a department's only part, selects the department. Hovering a block or a part shows its full name and counts line. Squares have no hover, no tooltip and nothing for screen readers: the waffle does not identify people. Its loading, error and empty states are the page's, as for the map.

The summary gives each department's counts (people on Lightdash, active in 30 days and in 12 weeks, the role split), not who is in which group, so `waffle/groupSquares.ts` makes each part's people from its counts: activity in order, with the roles spread evenly through it. Every colouring's counts are exact, but which square holds which role is not data, and no square stands for a particular person.

A change of colouring goes through `map/colourTransition.ts`. With `COLOUR_TRANSITION = 'sweep'`, the default, every place keeps its square and fades to its new colour over 380 ms once a diagonal front from the waffle's top-left corner reaches the square's centre, after (x + 0.6 y) / 2.2 ms, while the map's soft band crosses the waffle on the same timing. With `'reflow'` each person keeps their square, which glides to its new place over 640 ms (`cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)`) after 0.3 ms for every place before its old one, fading as it goes; people without an account stay where they are. The constant is the only switch, but a reflow of more than 2,000 squares in view (`REFLOW_SQUARE_LIMIT` in `waffle/groupSquares.ts`) sweeps instead, and its squares are then keyed by place, so none jumps to a new place before the sweep reaches it. New numbers or a resize during a change end it at once, selection and hover never animate, and people who prefer reduced motion see the colours change at once. Each part's squares are drawn by a memoised component, so a selection redraws no square.

## Known limits

- A placement lost outside the department writes, such as a person leaving a linked group in the groups settings, leaves their primary stored but ignored until the next department write in the organization clears it. If they are placed there again before that, it counts again.
- The snapshot reads the departments and the people in two queries, not one transaction. A department created or deleted between them can leave a placement outside the tree until the snapshot is rebuilt; that placement is never counted, ringed or named.
- Drawn from counts, which of a department's dots carry rings is not known, only how many. With names loaded the rings are on the right people.
- A parent's `sharedCount` includes a person in two of its own sub-departments, so hovering the parent can say "1 counts in another department too" for someone whose other department is inside it.
- The overlaps diagram shows at most three sets: the department and its two largest overlaps that are not above or below each other. The others are in the list beside it.
- A scheduled chart or dashboard run still writes a chart view (and possibly a dashboard view) under its owner, and those rows carry nothing that tells a scheduled run from a person. The weekly trend is built from views only, so a weekly schedule makes its owner show as active every week, and it can keep them active in the 30-day count too.
- `query_history` does not record whether a query came from a data app or a schedule that reused an interactive context, so such a run still counts as a query.
- When the cached snapshot has expired, the next read, including a single department page, loads the whole organization snapshot and passes every member uuid to SQL.
- The snapshot cache lives in each backend process, so a write handled by one process does not clear another process's copy; figures can stay up to 60 seconds old there.
- Users without a primary email, deactivated users and invited people who have not completed sign-up are left out of the member rows altogether. With no headcount set they are not visible anywhere on the page.
- The newest weekly bucket is the current, partial week, so it usually reads low.
- The weekly trend counts chart and dashboard views, not queries, so people who only query are missing from it.
- Queries are retained for a limited period set by the instance (`QUERY_HISTORY_RETENTION_DAYS`, 32 days by default). A retention below 30 days makes the 30-day active count, the per-person query count and top explores undercount.
- "No activity in 90 days", and Lost on the map, mean nothing recorded in the last 90 days. It covers people who never used the product, people last active more than 90 days ago, and people whose only activity was a query that has since been deleted.
- A headcount entered on a parent below its children's total (a person in several of them counted once) plus the people directly in it is not used: the parent counts that total instead, and the list and the department page say the headcount entered is below its sub-departments and its own people.
- Dashboards in a trashed space can appear in key content. Only the dashboard's own `deleted_at` is checked.
- Key content names are visible to anyone with the view scope, whatever their access to the space. Following a link still needs access to the content.
- The map hides person dots above 20,000 placements in view, a person counting once in each department they count in and each person without an account once, and says so under the legend: "Dots are hidden above 20,000 placements. Open a department to see its people".
- Names at rest are never moved apart: where two would overlap, the smaller circle's is left out, so in a crowded level some circles are named on hover only.
- The Waffle view draws every part as a bar above 20,000 people in view and says so under the legend.
- Reflow is costly at scale, because thousands of transform transitions run at once: measured in headless Chromium, gliding about 5,400 squares drops frames throughout, with tasks of 50 to 80 ms, and about 17,500 squares stall for about two seconds. So above 2,000 squares in view the waffle sweeps even when the constant says reflow. The sweep keeps the full frame rate at 5,400 squares, and at 17,500 its slowest frames take about 33 ms.
- Person dots are a picture of how a department is doing, not a way to find someone. The map never draws people's names: a dot names its person, and their part of the colouring, only on hover, selecting it opens their card, and keyboard users get the people listed by name. The map loads a department's people only when 150 or fewer are in view. Above that, dots are drawn from counts and cannot be selected; to find a person, use the department page's people list.
- The placement modal and the drawer's list of the department's people draw 50 people at a time, and the drawer's pickers offer 50 options at a time; search reaches the rest. The department detail response itself still returns every member, and opening the drawer from the index still loads the organization's whole membership list.
- Safari trackpad pinch does not zoom the map.
